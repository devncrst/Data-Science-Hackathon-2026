Why neighbours matter

How neighbours changed the guess

h4d2 first guesses each patch on its own, then blends that guess with its neighbours within 90 m. Below is a real Amsterdam window from the first official draw: the true ages (never shown to the model), the model's own guesses, and the blended guesses that were scored.

Does blending help? Macro F1 over the 10 official draws

The official scores are the blended ones. Blending costs a little when labels are scarce and helps at 200 labels; most of h4d2's gain comes from the Madrid prior and the networks.

06

How h4d2 works

Four parts, each moving what the model learned in Madrid over to Amsterdam.

1 · Neighbour context

Each patch's 60 temporal features and 42-year series are averaged with its 90 m neighbourhood.

2 · Six networks

1-D CNNs read the full Landsat series. Three are domain-adversarial: trained so they cannot tell Madrid from Amsterdam.

3 · Gentle adaptation

With n labels per class, each Madrid head is re-fitted while being pulled toward its Madrid weights.

4 · Smoothing

Final probabilities are blended with neighbouring patches, and the n known labels spread to their block. Section 04 shows exactly what this changes.

Disclosures

The domain-adversarial networks used unlabelled Amsterdam pixels (no labels). At prediction time the provided Amsterdam file serves as unlabelled neighbours. Settings were tuned on the provided Amsterdam data, so hidden-test scores may be slightly lower. Leakage test: shuffling every non-support label changes 0 predictions.
